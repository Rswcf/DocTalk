# Claude after the synthesis: one correction, one concession, one dissent (2026-09-22)

**Correction: ChatDOC's page limit is 300, not 3.** Re-read from chatdoc.com's live "Plans and Pricing" table in
the browser pane on 2026-09-22. The Free column reads, in order:

| Row | Free value |
|---|---|
| File upload limit | "5/day, 10 in total" |
| Question limit | "20/day, 100 in total" |
| Page limit per file | **"300"** |
| Max file size | "60 MB" |

"3 Pages/File" belongs to a later row, most likely "Translate File", not to the page limit. So
`20-synthesis-fable.md` §1 items 1–2, §6, and the first owner bullet ("3 pages per file") misread it, and so did
T1/T2.

What survives the correction:
- ChatDOC remains one of the three lifetime-cap tools (10 files and 100 questions, ever).
- DocTalk remains the most generous per document among tools that publish a per-file cap: 750 pages against
  300, 150, 120 and 100.

What does not survive: "demo-grade" now rests on the lifetime counts alone. PDF.ai's "1 PDF" stands, verified
on its live table.

**Concession: keep the monthly grant.** I withdraw 02 §4.1. Fable's argument is right:
- nobody has hit the credit allowance in seven months, so removing the grant has no revenue upside;
- it would rewrite 47 allowance keys × 11 locales, including FAQ JSON-LD;
- it only touches the returning users, the one cohort with any retention.

Resetting the grant instead of letting it accumulate is hygiene for a later batch, not a lever.

**Dissent: synthesis §2.4 uses the wrong reason against card-first trials.** "0 of 67 users were present on day
4, so nobody is there when a trial ends" is an argument against *opt-in* trials, which need the user to come back
and convert. For an *opt-out*, card-on-file trial it cuts the other way: absence means the charge goes through.

That is why card-required trials top the only per-visitor benchmark (10.5 payers per 1,000 visitors against 5 for
freemium, T3 row 18).

The rejection still stands, on the other three reasons:
1. **Signups fall about 60%.** On ~9 signups a month, that kills the learning signal.
2. **Charging absent users is exposed.** Their charges sit on 14-day withdrawal rights, the Sofatutor disclosure
   rule and the card-network reminders, and the product has no § 356a withdrawal function yet.
3. **The category promises "free".** Four of five English titles and five of five Spanish titles say so.

So the owner should hear this plainly: the "try, then you must pay" model the owner describes does exist in its
strongest form, a card-first trial, and it wins per visitor. We reject it for these reasons, not because
conversions cannot happen.

Everything else in the synthesis I agree with: the non-renewing pass beside Plus at every wall (for zh the only
payable path), the trial on the anonymous surface (§2.3), readable units, nothing before 09-28, and existence as
the metric.
